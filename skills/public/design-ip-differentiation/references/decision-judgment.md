# Differentiation decision judgment

Compare candidates through evidence and consequences, not a decorative score.

## Necessary questions

1. **Relevance** — Is the intended difference tied to a real public job or
   decision, or only interesting to the operator?
2. **Truth** — Does the entity own credible facts, mechanisms, rights, history
   or behavior that support it?
3. **Choice** — Would the difference change preference among real
   alternatives? What does the entity refuse in order to make that choice?
4. **Attribution** — Can stable signals make the association attach to this
   entity rather than to the topic, trend or benchmark?
5. **Generativity** — Can the dramatic engine produce at least ten materially
   different real events without resetting the premise?
6. **Operability** — Can the team sustain the evidence, production cost,
   cadence, rights and public exposure?
7. **Extensibility** — Can the association support future offers or formats
   without becoming generic?
8. **Falsifiability** — Is there a practical observation that would weaken,
   revise or retire the judgment?

## Reject a candidate when

- its choice reason is only an adjective or production style;
- the same direction fits several competitors after replacing the name;
- borrowed expression carries more distinctiveness than owned truth;
- it depends on guaranteed distribution, virality or an undisclosed platform
  weight;
- it creates attention but has no plausible route to trust, intent, adoption or
  economic value;
- it requires a fake persona, fabricated reality or unsustainable production;
- no observed result could disconfirm it.

## Tie-breakers

Prefer the candidate with stronger proprietary evidence, a clearer sacrifice,
more repeatable real event supply, lower dependency on platform luck and a
shorter path to an observable choice or adoption signal. If evidence is too
weak to choose, run the cheapest discriminating test; do not average
incompatible directions into a vague compromise.
